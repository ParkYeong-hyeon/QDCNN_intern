# Challenges and opportunities in quantum machine learning


**Source**: Cerezo et al., *NCS* (2022)
**Link**: https://doi.org/10.1038/s43588-022-00311-3

## Summary

This paper investigates the impact of <u>different data encoding strategies</u> on the performance of quantum-classical convolutional neural networks (QCCNNs), particularly in medical imaging applications. It explores why certain encoding methods outperform others by analyzing quantum metrics and the Fourier series decomposition of quantum circuits. The study highlights the critical role of <u>data encoding and re-uploading in QCCNN performance</u>, especially when dealing with <u>limited training data</u>, a common scenario in medical imaging. The authors find that while quantum metrics offer limited insights, Fourier coefficients analysis provides better clues for understanding the effects of data encoding.

## Key Contributions

### Analysis of Data Encoding Effects
- **Systematic Comparison of Encoding Schemes**: The study analyzes how four different data encoding methods (angle encoding around X- and Y-axis, higher-order, and amplitude encoding) affect QCCNN performance in binary and multi-class classifications of ultrasound and CT medical images.
- **Effect of Input Scaling and Data Re-uploading**:It also investigates the impact of input scaling and data re-uploading on the results.

### Correlation with Quantum Metrics
- **Performance–Metric Relationship**: The classification performance of all architectures is analyzed in relation to three key quantum metrics: expressibility, entanglement capability, and normalized effective dimension.
- **Identification of Correlated Indicators**: The study identifies which quantum metrics are structurally correlated with performance improvements, providing interpretability for architectural design choices.

### Fourier Formalism Application
- **Fourier-Based Circuit Output Analysis**: The analysis is extended by applying Fourier formalism to the outputs of the quantum circuits, enabling a spectral interpretation of different data encoding schemes.

- **Interpretation of Encoding-Induced Structure**: This approach provides theoretical insight into how data encoding methods shape the representational structure of circuit outputs.

## Methodology

### QCCNN Architecture
- The QCCNNs are constructed by replacing the first classical convolutional layer of a CNN with a variational quantum circuit (VQC), while keeping the rest of the architecture classical . All qubits are measured, and their outcomes are mapped to separate output channels, followed by a classical fully-connected layer for final classification using a sigmoid activation function .

### Encoding Strategies
- **Angle Encoding**: Data is encoded by rotating qubits around the X or Y axis, with `N` classical data entries requiring an `N`-qubit system  . The study uses two schemes: `Rx` gate and `Ry` gate .
- **Higher-Order Encoding**: This method was initially designed for Support Vector Machine (SVM) classifiers and involves a feature map unitary given by a specific formula, including Hadamard gates and first-order angle encoding with `Rz` gates, and pairwise second-order operations with `Rzz` gates .
- **Amplitude Encoding**: Classical data is represented as amplitudes of a quantum state, requiring `log2(N)` qubits for an `N`-dimensional vector, offering a reduction in required qubits  .

### Data Re-uploading
- The encoding scheme is repeated between different variational layers to enhance performance, a technique demonstrated to significantly improve results . The study compares performance when data is re-uploaded up to five times .

### Quantum Metrics
- **Expressibility**: Quantifies a circuit's ability to explore the Hilbert space, measured by the Kullback-Leibler divergence relative to an ensemble of Haar random states .
- **Entanglement Capability**: Measured by the Meyer-Wallach entanglement measure, it quantifies a VQC's ability to generate entangled states .
- **Effective Dimension (ED)**: Evaluates the information capacity of a statistical model using the Fisher Information Matrix (FIM) .

### Fourier Analysis
- The outputs of variational quantum circuits are observed to be expressible in terms of Fourier series . The paper analyzes the Fourier coefficients to understand the functional form generated by the quantum circuits .

### Datasets
- **BreastMNIST**: Contains 546 training and 78 validation images of 2D breast ultrasounds for binary classification (normal vs. malignant lesions) .
- **OrganAMNIST**: Consists of 1,000 training and 600 validation images of 3D abdomen CT scans for multi-class classification of 11 organs .

## Results


### Performance with Varying Input Scalings
- The validation accuracy dramatically decreases as the input scaling factor `f` grows beyond `π`, despite good training accuracy . A scaling factor of `π/4` was selected for all encodings to maximize accuracies .

### Performance with Varying Encodings
- With one encoding layer, higher-order and `Rx` encoders achieve the best validation accuracy on both datasets. `Ry` performs slightly worse, and amplitude encoding performs significantly worse, likely due to its use of fewer qubits (2 instead of 4) .
- With two layers, `Ry` encoding shows comparable or superior validation accuracies to other methods, while higher-order declines slightly, and `Rx` remains stagnant . Amplitude encoding benefits from additional re-uploading but remains sub-optimal .
- Adding three or more re-uploading layers leads to a saturation in accuracy, with results remaining constant .

### Quantum Metrics and Performance Correlation
- Most circuits exhibit low expressibility, but increased data re-uploading layers increase expressibility without necessarily improving validation accuracy . There is no clear correlation between expressibility and best achieved accuracy .
- Amplitude encoding shows lower entangling capabilities, as expected due to using only 2 qubits . For other architectures, high entanglement values are observed, but similar metric values for `Ry` (four-layer) and higher-order (two-layer) encodings show divergent performances .
- Normalized effective dimension (NED) shows a downward trend with increasing layers, except for `Ry` encoding, where an increase in NED from layer 1 to 2 coincides with a significant accuracy increase . These results suggest a saturation effect as the number of layers increases .

### Fourier Coefficients Analysis
- At one layer, `Rx` and higher-order encodings have more non-null frequencies than `Ry` encoding. `Ry` is limited to purely real values, whereas `Rx` and higher-order encodings allow for a mix of real and imaginary coefficients . This aligns with `Ry`'s poorer performance at one layer .
- Increasing data re-uploading layers for `Ry` and higher-order encodings increases the variance of Fourier coefficients and makes their values more mixed (real and imaginary), especially from one to two layers, indicating a saturation of adaptable degrees of freedom . The best `Ry` performance aligns with observed Fourier coefficient distributions and saturation of degrees of freedom .

In conclusion, the paper emphasizes that data encoding is a critical factor in QCCNN performance, particularly in medical imaging, and that while quantum metrics provide some insight, Fourier analysis offers a more robust understanding of encoding effects.
