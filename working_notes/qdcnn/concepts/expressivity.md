## Definition

**Expressivity** (or **expressibility**) refers to the ability of a quantum circuit to explore and represent different regions of the Hilbert space. It quantifies how well a parametric quantum circuit can approximate arbitrary quantum states or unitary operations.

## Core Idea

Expressivity measures the "reach" of a quantum circuit—how much of the quantum state space it can access. A highly expressive circuit can represent a wide variety of quantum states, while a less expressive circuit is limited to a smaller subset of the Hilbert space.

**Key distinction**: Expressivity is about the *potential* of a circuit, while **trainability** is about whether that potential can be *realized* through optimization. A circuit can be highly expressive but hard to train (e.g., due to barren plateaus).

## Mathematical/Theoretical Background

### Dimensional Expressivity
- **Definition**: The number of independent parameters actually needed in a parametric quantum circuit
- **Method**: [@Funcke2020] develops a method to analyze dimensional expressivity, identifying superfluous parameters and obtaining maximally expressive ansatz with minimum number of parameters
- **Symmetry effects**: Symmetries in the circuit can reduce expressivity by constraining the accessible state space

### Expressibility Measurement
- **KL divergence from Haar**: Expressibility is often measured as the Kullback–Leibler divergence between the distribution of states generated by the circuit and the Haar random state ensemble
- **Interpretation**: Lower KL divergence indicates higher expressibility (closer to uniform exploration of Hilbert space)

### Effective Quantum Dimension
- **Definition**: [@Haug2021] uses the geometric structure of the parameter space via the **effective quantum dimension** to reveal expressive power
- **Connection to capacity**: Effective quantum dimension relates to the circuit's capacity to represent different functions

## From Papers

### [@Funcke2020]: Dimensional Expressivity Analysis
- **Key finding**: Method to quantify how many independent parameters are actually needed in a parametric quantum circuit
- **Parameter reduction**: Technique to identify and remove redundant parameters while maintaining expressivity
- **Symmetry handling**: Discussion of how symmetries affect expressivity and methods to incorporate or remove them
- **Practical relevance**: Proof-of-principle demonstration on IBM quantum hardware using hybrid quantum-classical approach

### [@Haug2021]: Capacity and Quantum Geometry
- **Key finding**: Uses effective quantum dimension to evaluate capacity and trainability, revealing expressive power of circuits
- **Circuit type differences**: Striking differences in expressive power depending on type of entangling gates used
- **Scaling laws**: Particular circuits characterized by scaling laws in their expressiveness
- **Expressivity vs barren plateaus**: Finds region where circuit is expressive but doesn't suffer from barren plateaus, hinting at good initialization strategy
- **Parameter efficiency**: Algorithm to prune redundant parameters without affecting effective dimension

### [@Monnet2024]: Expressibility in QCCNN
- **Measurement**: Expressibility measured as KL divergence from Haar random state ensemble
- **Context**: Used as one of three key quantum metrics (along with entanglement capability and effective dimension) to analyze QCCNN architectures
- **Relationship to performance**: Analyzes relationship between expressibility and classification performance across different data encoding strategies

## In the Context of QDCNN

Expressivity is highly relevant to QDCNN's goals:

- **Architecture design**: Understanding expressivity helps design efficient quantum circuits that balance expressiveness with trainability
- **Long-range entanglement**: [@Haug2021] finds that entangling gate type affects expressiveness, which is relevant for QDCNN's use of long-range entanglement
- **Parameter efficiency**: Methods to analyze and optimize dimensional expressivity can help design QDCNN circuits with fewer parameters while maintaining capacity
- **Initialization strategies**: Finding regions where circuits are expressive but don't suffer from barren plateaus can inform QDCNN circuit initialization
- **Trade-offs**: Expressivity vs trainability trade-offs are crucial for QDCNN, as highly expressive circuits may be harder to train

## Key Insights

- **Expressivity ≠ Trainability**: A circuit can be highly expressive but difficult to train (e.g., due to barren plateaus). Expressivity measures potential, trainability measures realizability.
- **Entangling gates matter**: The type of entangling gates used significantly affects expressiveness [@Haug2021]
- **Dimensional expressivity**: Not all parameters contribute equally to expressivity; some may be redundant [@Funcke2020]
- **Symmetries reduce expressivity**: Circuit symmetries can constrain the accessible state space, reducing expressivity
- **Scaling laws**: Some circuit architectures show scaling laws in their expressiveness [@Haug2021]
- **Measurement methods**: Expressibility can be measured via KL divergence from Haar, effective quantum dimension, or dimensional expressivity analysis

## Notes and Questions

- **Expressivity vs Expressibility**: The terms are often used interchangeably, though "expressibility" is more common in quantum ML literature
- **Optimal expressivity**: What is the optimal level of expressivity? Too low → limited capacity; too high → may lead to barren plateaus
- **Initialization**: How can we initialize circuits to be in the "sweet spot" of high expressivity without barren plateaus?
- **QDCNN-specific**: How does QDCNN's dilated entanglement pattern affect expressivity compared to nearest-neighbor patterns?
- **Connection to effective dimension**: How does expressivity relate to effective dimension and capacity?
- **Practical measurement**: What is the most practical way to measure expressivity for QDCNN circuits?
